# 03 — Lore Bible

## 3.1 The rule for this chapter

Everything here is either **(R) real** — drawn from Igbo cosmology as actually
believed and practised — or **(G) game** — this project's invention, built on top
of the real material.

**No contributor may add a (R) entry.** If new lore needs a real deity, practice,
or word that is not in this glossary, it gets flagged and asked about, never
invented. Adding (G) material is fine and expected, and it must be marked.

## 3.2 The cosmology

**(R)** In Igbo belief, **Chukwu** (also Chineke) is the supreme being — remote,
uninvolved in daily affairs, not petitioned directly. Below Chukwu are the
**alusi** (also arụsị): deities with domains, shrines, priests and opinions.
Between the living and the alusi are the **ndichie**, the ancestors, who are
present and consulted. Each person also has a **chi**, a personal spirit-double
that carries their portion of destiny.

**(G) How this game uses it:** Chukwu is never named in-game and never appears.
The alusi are the powers you draw on (the four spells). The ancestors are the
dead you walk among. Your **chi** is the game's explanation for Onwe — when you
refused the cycle, your chi did not, and it kept walking the road you got off.

This is our single largest (G) liberty and it is the load-bearing one. It should
be treated as this game's interpretation and never presented as doctrine.

## 3.3 The alusi in this game

### Ala **(R)**
The earth. The most important deity in most Igbo communities. Owner of the land,
source of morality (crimes are **nsọ Ala**, abominations against Ala), and the one
who receives the dead into the ground.

**In game:** the ground itself. Her spell, **Njide Ala** (Grasp of Ala), roots
enemies where they stand. Her gift, **Ala's Fall**, is the earth agreeing to break
when you strike it. She is not your ally — she permits you.

### Amadioha **(R)**
Lightning and thunder; associated with justice, public retribution, and the sky.
The strike that settles a dispute.

**In game:** **Nkwenye Amadioha** (Assent of Amadioha) — nine bolts on everything
standing. The word "assent" matters: you do not command it, you are agreed with.

### Idemili **(R)**
A water deity of the Idemili region, associated with the river, with pythons
(sacred to her), and with purity.

**In game:** **Ọjii Idemili** (Veil of Idemili) — water stands between you and the
next wounds. The room Iyi Idemili is hers, and the water there *remembers*, which
is a (G) extension of her association with what is carried and kept.

### Ikenga **(R)**
Not a public deity but a personal one — a carved wooden figure with horns,
kept by a man to embody his right hand, his strength, his personal achievement.
Fed and honoured; broken and buried at death.

**In game:** **Aka Ikenga** (Hand of Ikenga) — your right hand answers to you
alone, briefly. The horned shape of the ikenga is the visual root of every horned
silhouette in the game. Mechanically it is a damage buff, and thematically it is
the most personal of the four: the others are borrowed, this one is *yours*.

## 3.4 The antagonists

### Ogbunabali **(R)** — "he who kills at night"
A real deity of death, who kills in the night, particularly those who have
committed offences. Worshipped in some communities; feared in all of them.

**(G) extension:** in this game he is *deniable*. He regenerates when unnamed
because a night-death is a thing that can always be attributed to something else —
illness, chance, bad luck. Naming him is the act of refusing that. Everything
mechanical about him grows from this one idea and it must be preserved in any
rework.

### Ekwensu **(R)** — the trickster/war spirit
A spirit associated with violence, trade, bargaining and trickery. Important
note for accuracy: **Ekwensu is not the Igbo devil.** That equation was made by
missionary translation and is contested and resented. In this game Ekwensu is
what it was before that: a spirit of war and hard bargains, who can be *hired*.

**This distinction is not optional.** Never write Ekwensu as satanic, never as
evil-for-its-own-sake. It was paid. It says so.

### Onwe **(G)** — "self"
Wholly this game's invention, built on the (R) concept of the chi. The version of
you that stayed in the cycle. It has your moveset, your height, your posture.

**Design rule:** Onwe must always mirror the player's *current* build in a future
rework — same weapon class, same spell tier. The horror of it is that it is not a
copy of a hero, it is a copy of *you specifically*.

## 3.6 The riddles

**(R)** *Gwam gwam gwam* riddles are a real and living Igbo children's and
storytelling tradition. The ten in the game are traditional forms.

**Rules for adding riddles:**
1. The Igbo must be grammatically correct. If unsure, ask — do not guess.
2. The answer must be a concrete noun a child would know. No abstractions.
3. All three options must be plausible; wrong answers are never jokes.
4. The English gloss is a *translation*, never an explanation.
5. Never write a riddle whose answer is a game mechanic.

Current set (answers): egg, water, fire, smoke, shadow, needle, the eyes, snail,
the slit drum, and the ọgbanje itself — which is the only one that is about the
player, and is deliberately last in the rotation.

## 3.7 Codex entry standards

The codex has two halves: **lore** (unlocked by story progress) and **bestiary**
(unlocked by killing the thing).

**Lore entry rules:**
- 40–80 words. One idea.
- Written in the world's voice, not a narrator's. It should read as though
  transcribed from someone who believes it.
- Never states a mechanic.
- Never gets ahead of the player. An entry may raise a question the player has
  not yet had answered, but must never answer one they have not yet asked.

**Bestiary entry rules:**
- 30–60 words.
- One sentence of what it is, one of how it behaves, one that is dry.
- Written by someone who has fought it and lived, i.e. the player-character's
  observation, but still never in first person.
- Must not state frame data. "It bites twice, and the second one gets you,
  because you were already walking away" is correct. "17-frame wind-up,
  parryable" is not.

Example of the target register, from the shipped game:

> **Arụsị Nche — the watching idol.** Carved, planted, and left with
> instructions. Its eyes are cowries. It cannot follow you, which it does not
> appear to consider a problem.
